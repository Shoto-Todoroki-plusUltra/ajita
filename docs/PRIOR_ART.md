## Current package and IBM documentation findings

**Maintenance risk and successor:** the official [Qiskit Optimization repository](https://github.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-optimization) says it was archived on 12 July 2026; [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/qiskit-optimization/) also marks it archived and lists 0.7.0. Its [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-optimization/main/README.md) explicitly says IBM no longer supports it and recommends `qiskit-addon-opt-mapper`. The first Phase 0 proposal missed that recommendation; the comparison below supersedes its archived-library-first integration strategy. Retain archived Optimization only as an optional historical comparator, not a required runtime dependency.

The [0.7 migration guide](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-optimization/migration/03_migration_guide_to_v0.7.html) relocates relevant eigensolvers/optimizers into `qiskit_optimization`, adds V2 primitive support and deprecates V1 use. Its examples require a pass manager for Aer V2, with statevector primitives excepted. Older `qiskit_algorithms`/SamplerV1 snippets are not the chosen design.

Current [IBM primitives documentation](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/qiskit-runtime-primitives) describes SamplerV2/EstimatorV2 and a newer beta Executor. Review the [SamplerV2 API](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit-ibm-runtime/sampler-v2), [transpilation guide](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/transpile) and [Compute service overview](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/guides/compute-services) at the later integration gate. Use target-compatible circuits and distinguish local primitives from IBM cloud services. Executor's existence does not justify adding beta integration now.

[IBM plans](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/plans-overview) vary by account and execution mode; [session documentation](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/run-jobs-session) says Open Plan users cannot submit session jobs. No plan eligibility, available credits, future cost or account access has been verified. Future hardware work requires explicit approval and a review of expected charges, even when nominally free access is available.

## Unsettled contribution and review limits

No practical quantum speedup demonstrated; novelty unverified. Generic QUBO conversion, QAOA, unified adapters, feasibility checking, resource reports and method comparison must not be advertised as novel. A possible future study is penalty robustness and feasibility rates under fixed original instances, resource caps and honest timing. A reproducible negative result is acceptable. A successful study would still need a targeted literature search before asserting originality.

Phase 3 must deepen the targeted preliminary review below before formulating a contribution claim. No exhaustive patent search, complete survey, installation of comparator systems, license audit or reproduction of published benchmarks was done in Phase 0. ## Optimization Mapper: features actually needed

Read the [official repository](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper), [documentation](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/), [migration guide](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/how_tos/00_migrate_from_qiskit_optimization.html), converter/API pages, installation instructions, release notes and selected source. This is a modeling/mapping successor, **not a drop-in quantum solver replacement**: the migration guide excludes former `algorithms`, `optimizers` and `minimum_eigensolvers` modules. `OptimizationProblem` replaces `QuadraticProgram` and extends polynomial modeling.

| Required capability | Mapper | Archived Optimization | Explicit encoder + Qiskit core | Choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Native exact/OR-Tools TSP and CP-SAT B1 | Does not supply our OR-Tools adapters; has CPLEX/Gurobi/SciPy reference interfaces | Classical wrappers do not replace our native baselines | Separate domain/classical package | Keep native baselines and independent oracle. No commercial solver needed. |
| Auditable depot-fixed 9-variable TSP polynomial | TSP application model exists; must check formulation and variable count | TSP application model exists; same qualification | Our explicit polynomial and decoder | Reference is ours; never substitute a library model silently. |
| Optional constrained-model to QUBO conversion | `OptimizationProblemToQubo`, explicit/automatic penalty, compatibility and `interpret` | `QuadraticProgramToQubo` and converter interpretation | Hand expansion only for bounded supported domains | Prefer isolated Mapper adapter after cross-check; no generic converter required for checkpoint 1A. |
| QUBO to Ising plus constant | `to_ising` returns operator and offset | Corresponding translation | `SparsePauliOp` plus tested algebra `x=(1-Z)/2` | Direct reference mapping; optional Mapper comparison. |
| QAOA circuit, optimization loop, V2 sampling | No QAOA/optimizer/eigensolver migration path | Integrated QAOA/MinimumEigenOptimizer, but unsupported package | `qaoa_ansatz`, V2 sampler and bounded SciPy loop | Direct core execution in checkpoint 1B; Mapper may supply mapping only. |
| Portable format, strict invalid-bit decoding and original feasibility | Converter interpretation is useful but insufficient | Same limitation | Our schemas/validators | Always owned/tested independently. |
| HUBO, continuous/general polynomial models, alternative commercial solvers | Broader modeling features available | More restricted quadratic modeling | Outside our bounded MVP | Not needed; no scope expansion or mandatory extras. |

The [QUBO converter API](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/stubs/qiskit_addon_opt_mapper.converters.OptimizationProblemToQubo.html) restricts supported inputs (notably continuous variables, higher-order objectives, quadratic/higher-order constraints and incompatible constraint coefficients). [Selected converter source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/main/qiskit_addon_opt_mapper/converters/optimization_problem_to_qubo.py) checks compatibility, chains conversions and interprets them in reverse. Model support is broader than QUBO-conversion support. Explicit penalty and variable-order normalization are required; automatic penalties are experimental comparison settings, not assumed adequate. The [Ising API](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/stubs/qiskit_addon_opt_mapper.translators.to_ising.html) exposes an offset separately; test it against all basis energies.

### Maintenance, maturity and compatibility

Mapper is IBM's recommended supported successor with an unarchived official repository and published releases; that is maintenance evidence, not proof of correctness or a future support guarantee. [Published package metadata](https://pypi.org/project/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/) and [release notes](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/release-notes.html) currently show 0.1.0; inspected [main-branch metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Qiskit/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/main/pyproject.toml) declares 0.2.0. Do not treat unreleased main as the installed release. Documentation has a November 2025 update date. No explicit beta/experimental classifier was found in the reviewed metadata; **early pre-1.0 maturity and API drift are our risk assessment**, not a vendor experimental-status label. The documented deprecation policy allows breaking changes.

PyPI lists Python >=3.10, Python 3.12 classifiers and a platform-independent wheel. Inspected main requires `qiskit<3` and includes NumPy, SciPy, CVXPY, NetworkX, rustworkx and Docplex. Release-specific dependency metadata was not verified: tag-based source fetches failed, and main cannot establish 0.1.0 requirements. Pin a published release only after inspecting its metadata and resolving its transitive native wheels on this arm64 Mac. CVXPY and its solver dependencies add a larger resolution surface than direct core mapping; do not install `dynamic`, commercial solver or developer extras by default. A pure-Python Mapper wheel does not certify all native dependencies or macOS 26.5.2 execution. No installation occurred.

Mapper application/converter APIs, release/main mismatches, automatic-penalty assumptions, floating-point tolerances and Pauli/string order remain compatibility risks. Our independently tested explicit encoder and domain validator remain authoritative if an adapter disagrees. An external model with 16 unreduced bits cannot be tested on 512 assignments as though it were the 9-bit reduced formulation: substitute the same depot constants and align variables first, or test its full space separately. Failures are reported, not patched by silently changing the problem.

## Targeted preliminary penalty/feasibility review

Searches covered penalty weights for permutation QUBOs, constrained QAOA, quantum school/railway timetabling, and formulation resources. The following primary sources were inspected; results from aggregators/social media were used only as discovery leads, not evidence. No study was reproduced.

| Source and review depth | Already known | Uncertainty relevant to our experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Ayodele, Penalty Weights in QUBO Formulations: Permutation Problems](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.11040v1) (EvoCOP 2022); selected introduction, weight methods and conclusion | TSP/QAP penalty selection, too-weak infeasibility and too-large search barriers are explicitly studied; Digital Annealer/CPU experiments compare static weights. | Results on these solvers do not establish the best weight for our finite-shot QAOA or B1. Reproduce formulas and distinguish sufficient global bounds from local-search heuristics. |
| [Hadfield et al., From the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm to a Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03489) (2017 preprint/2019 publication); abstract | Feasible-subspace mixers are established alternatives for hard constraints. | Their feasibility/resource trade-offs require different circuits; they are prior art, not an authorized Phase 1 implementation. |
| [Herman et al., Constrained Optimization via Quantum Zeno Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.15024v6) (2023); selected numerical/penalty-tuning sections | Portfolio experiments study non-monotonic penalty effects on feasibility and quality, and compare a different constraint-handling method. | This is not TSP/B1 evidence. Guarantees on ground states and probabilities under specific dynamics must not be transferred to our shallow QAOA. |
| [Grange et al., Quadratic versus Polynomial Unconstrained Binary Models for Quantum Optimization illustrated on Railway Timetabling](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10062v1) (2024 preprint); selected resource discussion and conclusion | Small railway examples compare PUBO/QUBO, discuss auxiliary-qubit/landscape costs and report formulation-dependent quality. | Railway timetabling differs from classroom B1; this does not prove PUBO superiority generally or justify adding HUBO to our MVP. |
| [Pires, A quantum heuristic for the school timetabling problem](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/xmlui/handle/123456789/231060) (UFSC, 2021); repository abstract | Two-stage graph-coloring/QAOA and classical soft-constraint handling has been proposed for school timetabling. | Complete thesis/results and relevance to our room/capacity model need later review; no performance claim adopted. |
| [Lucas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5843), official Mapper/Optimization converters and MQT Bench (above) | Penalty encodings, conversion and circuit resource benchmarking all have precedent. | End-to-end original-feasibility/resource reporting is valuable engineering, not established novelty. |

Known: sufficient penalties can preserve feasible ground-state optima under stated assumptions; this does not guarantee a heuristic samples feasible states. Penalty magnitude and formulation can affect search quality. Auxiliary variables and constraint structure affect resources. Merely increasing a nonzero penalty in a fixed Ising graph need not increase qubit count or entangling-gate topology; it can change coefficients/rotation angles. A compilation-resource effect must be measured under a fixed target/compiler, not presumed.

**Provisional research question:** For bounded, exactly verifiable symmetric TSP and classroom B1 instances, how do provably sufficient versus weaker or larger constraint penalties affect feasible-shot fraction, best feasible original-objective gap and compilation resources under fixed QAOA/optimizer budgets, and which observed effects persist across domains and cross-checked conversion paths?

Uncertain: whether smaller certified penalties help this sampler, whether any policy transfers across domains, and whether resources change once formulation/target are controlled. Possible contribution: a reproducible cross-domain characterization of failure modes, verified penalty bounds and resource/quality trade-offs, with raw failures included. It would require actual experiments, independent validation and deeper literature review; it is **not a novelty claim or a new penalty-selection algorithm**. [PHASE_PLAN.md](PHASE_PLAN.md) defines controls and a smaller TSP-only fallback if timetabling evidence cannot be completed before the application timeline. Nature's publisher fetch and one railway HTML revision failed; author-hosted arXiv HTML was used instead. Verma/Lewis was identified through Ayodele but its publisher full text was unavailable, so its methods are a later primary-review item rather than a reproduced baseline.
